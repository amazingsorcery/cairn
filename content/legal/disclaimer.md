# Disclaimer

This page sets out the limits of what {{BRAND_NAME}} tells you and what you may rely on it for.
It is part of the [Terms of Service](/legal/terms).

We have written it at length rather than in a box of capital letters, because the limits are real
and a user who does not understand them will misuse the service.

## 1. Cataloguing is not endorsing

The presence of a tool in our catalogue means one thing: we believe it exists and we have
described it as accurately as we could on the date shown in its `lastVerifiedAt` field.

It does **not** mean:

- that the tool is lawful for you to use, in your jurisdiction, for your purpose;
- that it is accurate, reliable, or currently working;
- that it is safe to use, or that its operator handles your queries responsibly;
- that we have any relationship with, or receive anything from, its operator;
- that we recommend it.

Several entries exist principally as warnings. Some describe tools whose legality is actively
contested, whose operator is in a jurisdiction that should give you pause, or that will notify
your subject that you are looking for them. Read the `legalNotes` and `reliabilityNotes` fields
before using anything. They are the part of an entry that matters most and the part users skip.

## 2. Our editorial assessments are opinion

Three fields on every entry are our editorial judgement, not fact:

- **`reliability` (1–5)** is our assessment of how much weight a result from that tool should
  carry. It reflects coverage, freshness, false-positive behaviour and the interpretive hazards of
  the output. Reasonable practitioners will disagree with some of our ratings.
- **`confidence`** describes how sure *we* are about the accuracy of *our own entry* — not how
  good the tool is. `verified` means we checked a primary source during our last review;
  `needsReview` means we know something material is uncertain and we have said what.
- **`legalNotes`** is editorial research into the legal and ethical considerations around a tool.
  It is **not legal advice** — see section 4.

## 3. Accuracy and staleness

We take accuracy seriously. Our editorial standard and our verification log are published in the
repository so you can see what we checked and what we did not. Every entry carries the date its
claims were last confirmed and an explicit confidence value.

Even so:

- **Tools change without notice.** Pricing moves, free tiers close, features are withdrawn,
  services are acquired and shut down. This has happened repeatedly to once-canonical OSINT tools,
  and some of them are still recommended in training material years after they died.
- **An entry is a snapshot.** Read the `lastVerifiedAt` date. An entry verified months ago may be
  wrong today, and one dated to our knowledge horizon rather than a live check was not
  independently confirmed at all.
- **We may simply be wrong.** If you find an inaccuracy, tell us at {{BRAND_CONTACT_LEGAL}} — we
  will check it and correct it, and correcting the catalogue is the highest-priority editorial
  work we do.

**Verify anything you rely on, against the tool's own primary source, before you act on it.** This
applies with particular force to pricing, to licensing terms, and to any claim about legality.

## 4. Nothing here is legal advice

{{BRAND_NAME}} is not a law firm and no one who wrote this content is your lawyer.

The `legalNotes` on catalogue entries, the "before you start" sections in playbooks, and every
statement on this site about data protection, computer misuse, sanctions, defamation or
surveillance law are **general editorial research**. They are:

- not tailored to your facts;
- not necessarily current;
- not comprehensive;
- and frequently jurisdiction-specific in ways we cannot resolve for you, because the law that
  governs open-source research depends on where you are, where your subject is, who you work for,
  what you intend, and what you publish.

The same applies to sanctions and compliance content. Screening a name against a list is not a
compliance function, and this service cannot substitute for one.

**Obtain qualified legal advice in your own jurisdiction before conducting research that may
engage data protection, surveillance, computer misuse or defamation law.** If a decision matters,
it needs a lawyer, not a catalogue entry.

## 5. Playbooks describe method, not outcome

Our playbooks describe how experienced practitioners approach a class of problem. They are written
to be honest about failure, and that honesty is the point:

- **Following a playbook does not produce a verified result.** Most geolocations fail. Most
  attempts to establish a photograph's origin end with the origin unestablished. Most ownership
  traces terminate in an opaque jurisdiction.
- **A negative result is a real result** and is treated as one throughout. "We could not establish
  this" is a legitimate and publishable outcome, and a playbook that never produced one would be
  lying to you.
- **The playbooks assume judgement.** They tell you what to look for and how you will fool
  yourself. They cannot tell you whether your evidence is sufficient in your particular case.
- **We are not responsible for your conclusions.** If you publish an identification, an
  attribution or an allegation, that is your editorial and legal decision, made against your own
  standards and your own legal advice.

## 6. What we do not do

To restate the boundary, because it is the most commonly misunderstood thing about this service:

- We hold **no data about your investigation subjects.** No person index, no entity records, no
  dossiers.
- We **do not run queries for you.** Nothing you type into a workspace reaches any third-party
  tool.
- We do **no facial recognition or biometric matching**, and we will not add it.
- We **do not host or search breached credentials.** We catalogue services that report the fact of
  exposure; we will never return credential material.
- We use **no third-party trackers, advertising networks or behavioural analytics.**

If you need any of those things, this is not the product, and the reasons are set out in the
[Acceptable Use Policy](/legal/acceptable-use) and the [Privacy Notice](/legal/privacy).
